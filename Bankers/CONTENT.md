PART 1 — THE ACCOUNT (content view)
=====================================================================
- Bankers (@bankers): Instagram Reels only, about 360K followers. US dollars and US markets by default.
- Each video is a NEWS POST: one current, dated event that carries one evergreen teach.
- Subject: headline events in banking, finance, the stock market and individual companies.
- The client's named interests: what the big banks are doing (JPMorgan etc.), rate decisions and FOMC meetings, upcoming market
  events, new IPOs, and major companies, public or private (e.g. OpenAI).
- Audience: men aged 20–50 who invest in stocks.
- Tone: clean, polished, calm, educational. No hype, alarm, cynicism or catchphrase. No spoken sign-off.
- Goal: a consistent ~30K views per reel. Length near one minute, with strong hooks.
- HARD CLIENT RULE, no bias of any kind:
  - politically neutral; no take on real people;
  - no investment advice, no buy/sell calls, no price targets;
  - the honest part gives both sides equal weight;
  - don't moralise at the audience (e.g. never "don't play the lottery").
- Leave sensitive real-person stories out unless the video is about them. Example: the Nvidia earnings video left out the CEO
  change and the board investigation entirely.
- The voice is "we/us", never "I/me", on screen and in the VO.
- Categories (state one in every plan):
  - RECORD HUNTER (~8/month): a "highest/lowest since [year]" record or milestone.
  - CALENDAR ENGINE (~5/month): economic or financial calendar events (CPI, jobs, FOMC, sentiment, Real Earnings). Not company news.
  - CULTURE BRIDGE (~2/month): a famous brand or company story, or everyday money (lottery, prices), that carries a market lesson.
  - COMPANY: company-specific videos, when Timur asks.
- Avoid topics that overlap with current 100bps videos (Timur's other brand). Ask if unsure.
- Topics excluded by results: crypto (the Binance reel got 692 views).
- Don't repeat a company or topic covered in the last ~10 videos (check Videos/log.md). Example: Meta was refused because there had
  just been a Meta video.
- Never put prices, payments, contract terms or client contact details in any file you write.

PART 2 — IDEAS (when Timur asks for new ideas)
=====================================================================
1. Research the news with web search: current, verifiable and dated, with primary sources available.
2. Topic Demand: prefer topics people are already searching for or talking about, such as earnings everyone saw, famous brands,
   record numbers and everyday money. Check what competitors' reels did with similar topics:
   - Yahoo Finance, CNBC, WSJ, Morning Brew, @100basispoints;
   - known hits: Musk 844K, Morning Brew "October 29ths" 863K, 100bps "Bob" 965K, gold 508K, Nike 374K, WSJ Monopoly 279K.
3. Check our own grid (@bankers reels tab) in Claude in Chrome for view counts.
   - Compare videos AT THE SAME AGE, never raw totals. A reel posted that morning isn't a flop at 2,500 views.
   - Get the posting time from the reel shortcode: decode base64 (alphabet A–Z a–z 0–9 - _) to an integer id;
     timestamp_ms = (id >> 23) + 1314220021721.
   - Read captions from each reel page's meta description.
   - Don't call Instagram's private API (it gets blocked). Use the visible grid and pages.
4. What has worked for Bankers so far:
   - everyday money with a famous headline number: the Powerball $847M reel, 222K;
   - whole-market paradoxes;
   - "since [long-ago year]" records;
   - famous names with a contradiction between the stock and the business (e.g. Tesla);
   - anticipation hooks where something visibly shrinks, sinks or tears away.
5. Deliver 1–3 or more ideas. Each idea gets:
   - its category;
   - the hook (line 1 plus kicker);
   - the hook mechanic in one line (what moves on screen in the first 3 s);
   - the key facts with sources;
   - the teach;
   - why it should work (precedent and demand);
   - a "Hook chart/object:" line naming the real data or object behind the hook.
6. ALWAYS include a comparison table:
   Idea | Category | Hook | Hook mechanic | Demand signal | Precedent (reel + views) | Freshness | Data strength | Risk | Rating /10
   Then give a one-line recommendation.
7. If Timur says "drop X" or "decrease the weakest", eliminate one at a time and explain why that one was weaker.
8. Wait for his pick. Don't write the full plan until he picks (unless the task already holds an approved script or idea).
9. Anticipation hooks ("similar to Powerball"): keep the SAME IDEA (a big headline number, then watching how much survives) but use a
   DIFFERENT CONSTRUCTION every time. Already used:
   - Powerball: ball plus cash tower plus a slicing blade;
   - Nvidia: a block sinking into a cracking floor;
   - Lottery: a ticket slab tearing.
   Never reuse the previous video's hook construction or hero object (check log.md).

PART 3 — FROM AN APPROVED IDEA OR SCRIPT TO A FINAL SCRIPT
=====================================================================
The approved idea or script you receive may contain things that break the rules. Fix them and say what you changed. Examples
from past briefs:
- a map of a state → maps are banned, so make it a fill or bars;
- a split the source doesn't give → don't put separate figures on screen;
- "next week" or "just" → relative time is banned.

Research and accuracy:
- Use ONLY information that already exists. Never plan a video that has to wait for an event and fill numbers in later.
- Verify every live figure with a fresh search on the day you script. Cite primary sources: BLS, the Fed, company 8-Ks or press
  releases, the Census Bureau, Freddie Mac, official lottery sites, etc.
- ONE-DATASET rule: every set of compared figures comes from one dataset. Every on-screen number has a named source.
- Run all arithmetic and make sure bridges add up.
  - Nvidia: 115.8 − 29.2 − 9.2 − 13.7 + 1.5 = 65.3.
  - Anthropic: $72.4B + $0.7B didn't reach $80.7B until the line "most of the rest was similar paper marks" was added, which
    Daloopa supported.
- Percentages computed from a source are labelled as computed in the facts list.
- If a claim can't be confirmed, write a fallback line, use it, and tell Timur.
- When the wording is loose (e.g. "Americans spent" for state lottery sales), consider the stricter version and note it.

PART 4 — SCRIPT RULES
=====================================================================
Length:
- Hard cap 80 s, end card included. Aim near one minute.
- 175–190 words of VO. ElevenLabs "Jon – Calm Presence" at speed 1.10 reads about 2.8 words/s (185 words ≈ 65–70 s).
- Count words with a script every time.

Sentences:
- Full declarative sentences, one caption per screen.
- Numbers are spelled out in the VO: "one hundred four point seven billion", "fifty-six cents", "A-T-and-T", "fiscal twenty
  twenty-four", "two thousand eight".

NO RELATIVE TIME:
- Banned: just, now, right now, today, yesterday, tomorrow, this/next/last week, this month, last quarter, last year, a year ago,
  over the past year, recently, soon.
- Anchor to dates or periods: "On November seventeenth", "In the year to September", "A year earlier", "in the quarter to January".
- The only allowed "now" is the fixed line "Now, the honest part."
- Grep every script before saving.

Structure (labels for your files; they're never spoken):
[HOOK]
- ≤14 words plus a kicker. Present tense. The claim or contradiction comes in the first sentence, never a soft "let's look at…".
- The winning pattern: something everyone knows (the S&P 500, JPMorgan, Nvidia, paychecks, the lottery) plus ONE twist (a
  contradiction, a smaller company overtaking a giant, a "since [year]" record made relatable, a big number and then the part that
  survives).
- Never two ideas, never an odd yardstick, never a ranked statistic ("2nd lowest in 74 years"). Use plain-memory labels ("highest
  since 2007").
[WHAT HAPPENED]
- Dated, sourced, one new figure per line.
[THE TEACH]
- Opens "Here's what almost nobody understands."
- One concept (profit margin, expected value, present value…), framed as a discovery, never a warning.
- A comparison on the same scale works well (e.g. Nvidia 56¢ vs Apple 27¢ per $1).
[THE HONEST PART]
- Opens "Now, the honest part."
- One sentence per side, equal weight, about 6–7 s.
[AUDIENCE QUESTION]
- "So be honest. … Tell us below." Confession or opinion style.
[CTA]
- The house close. NOTHING spoken.
- NEVER "Follow for more market breakdowns." That's the 100bps sign-off and it's banned on Bankers. The VO ends on the question.

Save as Videos/Video N — <title>/VO_SCRIPT.txt:
- paste-ready text only: no labels, no notes;
- paragraphs separated by blank lines;
- numbers spelled out.
Timur records it himself in ElevenLabs (Jon – Calm Presence, speed 1.10). Never spend ElevenLabs or Higgsfield credits unless he
says to.

PART 5 — VIDEO STRUCTURE (the plan; write Videos/Video N/PLAN.md before building)
=====================================================================
The content chat used to send this as a prompt. You write it for yourself so the content intent is fixed before you build. Use this
template:

Category: [..] · same family as: [precedent reel + views] · hook mechanic: [..] · [date]
Title · target runtime · composition name
★ STYLE PRECEDENT: the hero-object spine (as in Video 21, Tesla). One hero object acts in the hook and returns through the film,
  doing something new each time.
★ RUNTIME / ★ TIMING: what's published and when. Nothing waits on a future event.
★★★ CONTENT RULES:
- every beat gets its own visual;
- the hook visual literally matches the words;
- compared pairs sit on one scale;
- labels of ≤4 words;
- no dead stretches;
- the ONE-DATASET list;
- neutrality notes.
★ HOOK: line plus kicker.
★★ HOOK SCENE: beat by beat, synced to the VO words. Frame 0 → line 1 → payoff on line 2.
★ SCOPE: one event, one teach, the two sides of the honest part.
SCRIPT: with section labels, one caption per screen.
CONTENT-LEVEL VISUAL NOTES: for every sentence, the object or chart, what it does, and its labels.
★ FACTS & VERIFY: every figure, its exact source and date, the arithmetic, and fallbacks.
DATA: the real series to plot from. Never hand-draw data.

Hook rules (from Timur's feedback):
- Hooks MUST MOVE from frame 0, continuously. Never "assemble, then sit".
- The hook opens on a fast push-in from far out.
- One recognizable hero object, detailed 3-D where the idea needs depth, with the action happening on it.
- Anticipation: the viewer waits to see how much survives, and the tension starts on frame 0.
- The payoff is the BIGGEST motion. It lands on line two, is large on screen, and reads for at least 1 s before any transition.
- Figures are printed on the moving object, early in its move. About 6 elements at most.
- Two subjects are shown one at a time, with a pan between them.
- No dials or gauges in the hook.
- Never the previous video's hook construction or hero object.
- Chart-first hooks (a line to a record with a glowing tip, a tiny bar vs a giant bar, a price line riding the move) still suit
  Record Hunter and Calendar Engine stories. Hero-object anticipation is the current default for company and everyday-money stories.

Body rules:
- No text-only scenes; every sentence gets a visual built from its own data.
- Most scenes carry ONE real object the sentence names, acting in the scene, in a rich composition, never "one plain object and a
  number".
- The visual ENACTS the sentence. Hershey example: "doubled profits" = the bar cut in half from the bottom, not "2x" printed on it.
  Discount figures like "10% off" go in the caption chip, not printed on the hero.
- ONE of each chart kind per film at most (bars, line, candles, pie, dial, steps, grid, fill, counter). NO MORE THAN ONE BAR SCENE.
  - The Anthropic video was sent back for 9–10 bar scenes and a static hook. Never repeat that.
  - Balance real objects and charts, never more than two chart beats in a row.
- Logos are visible and belong to the construction.
- Never maps or state outlines, people, document cards or phone mockups, etc. (STYLE.md has the full list).
- The close is fixed: the question as tracking text, then the BANKER$ close.

PART 6 — CAPTION (save as Videos/Video N/CAPTION.txt; in chat always show it in a code block so blank lines survive pasting)
=====================================================================
Format, in order, with a blank line between blocks:
1. The hook as the first line (figures as numerals: "$104.7 billion").
2. 3–4 short paragraphs: what happened (dated), the proof, the teach.
3. A paragraph starting "To be fair, …" (the honest part, both sides).
4. The question + " Tell us in the comments."
5. "Sources: <source, date>; <source, date>. Educational content, not financial advice."
6. "Follow @bankers for more."
7. At most 5 hashtags on one line. No emojis.
Rules:
- Numerals in the caption, spelled-out numbers in the VO.
- The caption must match the VO as RECORDED. After the final cut, check the finished video and update the caption to any changed
  lines (this was a standing task).
- No relative time here either.

Example (the lottery video, Nov 19):
```
Americans spent $104.7 billion on lottery tickets in one year. About a third never came back as prizes.

Census Bureau data for fiscal 2024 show state lottery ticket sales nearly doubled from $52.8 billion in fiscal 2008. Prizes paid back $70.2 billion. The other $34.5 billion stayed with the lotteries.

That split is expected value. On average, every dollar spent on tickets paid back about 67 cents, so a ticket is worth less than it costs before a single number is drawn. It varies by state: Virginia paid back 80 cents of every dollar, the most in the country. And jackpots grow only when nobody wins them, like the $847 million Powerball won on November 4.

To be fair, many states put their share toward schools, and a ticket buys a little fun. But on average, the math favors the lottery.

Do you buy a ticket when the jackpot gets big? Tell us in the comments.

Sources: US Census Bureau, state lottery data for fiscal 2008 and 2024 (April 2026); Powerball (November 5, 2026). Educational content, not financial advice.

Follow @bankers for more.

#lottery #powerball #money #personalfinance #investing
```
