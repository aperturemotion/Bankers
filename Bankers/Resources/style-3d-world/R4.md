Transcript as read from captions:
"GTA 6 is probably already on your console, all 212 gigabytes of it, and it won't open until one tiny key shows up at midnight. Analysts expect 25 million sales on day one, and this time there's no disc. Even the boxed copy is just a download code. Imagine all of them hitting download at the same midnight. That's over 5 [million] terabytes at once. The download servers would be swamped, and everyone's copy would crawl. So Rockstar opened preloading nine days [early], and on PS5 it started at midnight in each time zone, one region at a time, so the servers never face the whole world at once. In 2013, GTA 5 came on discs, two of them on the Xbox 360. Then GTA Online opened two weeks later, and the servers fell over. GTA 6 skips that night. There's no GTA Online at launch, so there's no giant lobby to crash. So how can the game be on your console and still be locked? Every file arrives encrypted, scrambled into noise. Your console stores it, but can't read a single frame. At midnight your time, your console checks in and gets a license with the key inside. The game is 212 gigabytes. The key would fit in a text message. So at launch, 25 million people aren't downloading a game. They're downloading a key. And because it's midnight local time, New Zealand plays before almost anyone, and the US waits most of a day. How long did your preload take?"

## 2. Hook and ending

**Hook (0–3 s):** opens on the most familiar possible frame (a living room with the GTA VI TV and a PS5) and a provocative claim. "GTA" appears alone, boxed in yellow (0.0–0.8), then "6 is probably / already on your console," while the camera dollies toward the console; at 2.2 the camera pushes into the PS5 and its shell turns to glass, revealing the hidden data slab by 3.5 — the "x-ray of something you own" is the visual hook.

**Ending (81–86.2 s):** globe time-zone payoff (+21 h for the US), then a return to the opening object (x-ray PS5 in the room) for a direct question to the viewer: "How long did your preload take?" (comment bait). Follow card fades/blur-scales in at ≈85.0 (fully readable 85.3), cursor clicks at 85.55 → "Following"; holds 0.7 s to the end.
