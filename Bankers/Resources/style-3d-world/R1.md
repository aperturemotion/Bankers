"Mercedes just built a headlight that cuts a dark hole around your car. It's on the new S-Class, and it keeps its high beams on while the driver coming toward it doesn't get blinded. And there's [a catch:] drivers in the US almost never got this. Imagine a dark country [road]. Your high beams are on, and a car comes over the hill. Normally you dip them, and the road ahead goes black. The S-Class doesn't dip. A camera behind the windshield spots the other car's [lights] and works out exactly where it is. That works because the headlight isn't one big bulb. It's a grid of about 25,000 tiny LEDs, like the pixels on a TV, and each one can [switch] on its own. So it turns off only the pixels that would hit the other driver. Everything else stays in full high beam, reaching nearly 2,000 feet, and the dark hole follows the car until it passes. But didn't the S-Class already do [this]? It did, by bouncing light [off] 1.3 million tiny mirrors. The new one throws the mirrors out and makes the [light] in the pixels themselves, and Mercedes says it lights an area about 40 percent bigger with up to half the energy. So why did the US miss out? Until 2022, US rules didn't allow high beams shaped like this, and with the new S-Class, Mercedes is switching this partial high [beam on] in the US for the first time. So the light never went away. It just went around the one car in the dark."
