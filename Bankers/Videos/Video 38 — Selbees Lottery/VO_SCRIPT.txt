In this town of just nineteen hundred and three people, a retired couple found a way to beat the lottery. Legally. For nine years.
In two thousand three, retired store owner Jerry Selbee read a brochure for a new Michigan game, Winfall. He found the loophole in three minutes.
When the jackpot hit five million and nobody won, the money rolled down to the smaller prizes. On those days, enough tickets put the math on your side.
Jerry tried it. Thirty-six hundred dollars in. Sixty-three hundred back.
He and his wife Marge formed a company with family and friends, and played twelve roll-downs, until Michigan ended the game.
Then they found a similar game in Massachusetts. For six years, they drove nine hundred miles a trip, often betting over six hundred thousand dollars.
They weren't alone. A group started by MIT students played it too. In twenty eleven, the Boston Globe broke the story, and many suspected a rigged game.
The state investigated. The betting was legal, and no one's odds were hurt. The state itself made one hundred twenty million dollars from it.
In all, their company won over twenty-six million dollars, nearly eight million of it profit before tax. Theirs went to family.
Sometimes the edge isn't luck. It's reading the rules.
