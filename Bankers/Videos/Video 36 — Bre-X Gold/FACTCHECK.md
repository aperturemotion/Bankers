## Line by line

### 1. HOOK: "Sixty-one thousand dollars of gold dust. That's all it took to build a six billion dollar company."

**$61,000: UNCONFIRMABLE.**
- The earliest dated appearance I could find is a Visual Capitalist infographic timeline, republished by Mining.com on Jan 25, 2015: "Over the next 2.5 years, de Guzman would buy $61k of panned gold from locals to use in salting." It cites no source and gives no currency. Wikipedia, the UBC course wiki, NowIKnow and similar pages all copy it.
- I found no trace of the figure in the Strathcona report as quoted at the time, the Freeport 8-Ks, the OSC charges, the Reuters, AP, CSM or Maclean's coverage, the CBC report on the Forensic Investigative Associates (FIA) findings, or the Globe and Mail's Felderhof-trial coverage. A search for "Busang" together with "61,000" returns nothing relevant.
- Whether it was US$, C$ or converted rupiah cannot be determined. The books the brief names (Danielson & Whyte, *Bre-X: Gold Today, Gone Tomorrow*, 1997; Goold & Willis, *The Bre-X Fraud*, 1997) could not be checked online. If someone has a page reference from one of them, it can come back.

**C$6 billion peak value: CONFIRMED (C$).**
- Maclean's, May 19, 1997: "Once valued by the market at $6 billion."
- Christian Science Monitor, May 7, 1997: "$6 billion (Canadian; $4.4 billion US)". The CSM wording "raise $6 billion" is loose; it means market value.
- AP (David Crary), May 9, 1997: "peak value of $4.5 billion last year". That is US$, and the peak was in 1996.
- Reuters, July 31, 2007: shares "had soared to value the firm at C$6 billion."
- One higher figure exists: the FT story reprinted by Business Standard on May 8, 1997 says "valued at C$6.8 billion" (text cut off). "Six billion" is the conservative, majority figure.
- **On-screen:** `C$6 BILLION`, sub-label `peak market value, 1996 · ≈US$4.4B`.

**Corrected hook (recommended):** "Gold dust, sprinkled into rock samples. That's all it took to build a six billion dollar company." The CSM, May 7, 1997, wrote: "Someone sprinkled or 'salted' gold flecks into original drill core samples."

**Fallbacks:**
- B. "A few pounds of gold dust. That's all it took…" AP, May 6, 1997, wrote: "only a small amount of gold — perhaps a few pounds — would have been enough." That is analysts' guess, so label it `analysts' estimate` if used.
- C. Keep "$61,000" only if a primary or book source with a page number turns up.

### 2. "In 1993, a tiny Calgary miner called Bre-X bought land in the jungle of Borneo, in Indonesia. By 1997, it claimed seventy million ounces of gold were in the ground."

- **1993 and Busang: confirmed.** The dates differ by source:
  - Reuters chronology, Feb 17, 1997: "March [1993] – Small Calgary, Alberta-based exploration company Bre-X Minerals Ltd starts gold exploration in Busang."
  - Maclean's timeline, Mar 3, 1997: "May 6, 1993 — Bre-X purchases control of the Busang gold project."
  - The Militant, June 23, 1997: "acquired the Busang site in early 1993."

  The line says only "1993", which is safe. "Bought land" is loose: Bre-X bought *control of a gold project*, meaning exploration rights, not land.
- **Calgary: confirmed.** Maclean's: "Bre-X Minerals Ltd. of Calgary". AP, Mar 28, 1997: "a tiny exploration company based in Calgary, Alberta". CSM: "a tiny Calgary mining company".
- **The ounces: needs correction.** Bre-X's own Feb 17, 1997 press release claimed **70.95 million ounces**. Sources:
  - The OSC charge notice of May 11, 1999 quotes the release: "totalling 70.95 million ounces of gold."
  - Reuters, Feb 17, 1997: up "by 13.6 million ounces to 70.95 million."
  - Maclean's, May 19, 1997: "upping the Kilborn calculations to 71 million ounces from 57 million."

  Earlier claims were 39.15M oz (June 20, 1996), 46.92M (July 22, 1996) and 57.33M (Dec 3, 1996). The **200M oz** figure was Felderhof saying he was "comfortable" with it on a Feb 19, 1997 analyst call (Maclean's). That was talk, not a company estimate, so leave it out.
- **Corrected line:** "In nineteen ninety-three, a tiny Calgary miner called Bre-X bought a jungle gold project in Borneo, Indonesia. By nineteen ninety-seven, it claimed seventy-one million ounces."
- **On-screen:** `70.95 MILLION OZ` with `Bre-X release · Feb 17, 1997`.

### 3. "The stock went from pennies to a six billion dollar company. Pension funds piled in."

- **Pennies: confirmed.**
  - AP, May 6, 1997: "investors drove up the price of a Bre-X share from pennies in 1993 to more than $200 last year." That $200 is the pre-split price; the stock split 10-for-1 on May 22, 1996.
  - Maclean's, May 19, 1997: the company "was once worth barely 10 cents a share."
  - The Militant, June 23, 1997: before 1993 the shares sold "on the Calgary stock market [Alberta Stock Exchange] for as low as two cents."
  - The brief's "~C$0.30 in 1993" is **not confirmed**. Don't put a 1993 price on screen; "PENNIES" is safe.
- **Pension funds: confirmed.** Maclean's, May 19, 1997, "A sampling of funds and their losses" (C$):
  - Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan Board, $100M
  - Caisse de dépôt et placement du Québec, $70M. Name it this way: the Caisse "manages the pension funds of public sector workers in Quebec" (The Militant). Wikipedia's "Quebec Public Sector Pension fund" is a description, not the fund's name.
  - Ontario Municipal Employees Retirement Board (OMERS), $45M
  - Newfoundland Public Sector Pension Plan, $5M
  - Halifax Regional Municipality fund, $1.5M
  - Edmonton Telephone Endowment Fund, $0.75M

  Why they piled in: the TSE added Bre-X to the TSE 300 index, so index-tracking pension funds bought it automatically (The Militant, citing the Globe and Mail).
- **Keep as written.**

### 4. "Then, on March 10th, 1997, a mining partner drilled its own test holes. It found an insignificant amount of gold."

- **Date: needs correction.** Freeport-McMoRan's 8-K (event date **March 26, 1997**) says: "During the past three weeks, FCX has drilled seven core holes within the Busang II project area … to confirm the results of core holes previously drilled by BRE-X … analyses … indicate insignificant amounts of gold." So the drilling ran through early to late March, and the finding was made public on March 26.
  - Maclean's, May 19, 1997, quotes Freeport's chairman, Jim Bob Moffett: "On March 12, Moffett phoned Toronto" to tell Bre-X "we had run into difficulties."
  - The Reuters chronology in Stockex has "March 27 – Freeport releases first results of drill holes twinning Busang holes." The holes were about 1.5 m from Bre-X's.
  - "March 10" comes only from Wikipedia, citing CityNews (July 31, 2007), which I could not retrieve. No primary source supports it.
- **Quote:** the exact words are "**insignificant amounts of gold**" (plural), in the 8-K of March 26, 1997 and AP of Mar 28, 1997.
- **Market reaction (context):** AP, Mar 28, 1997: "plummeted 84 percent to 2.50 Canadian dollars … down from 15.50 Canadian dollars."
- **Corrected line, restructured with line 5:** "That March, a partner drilled its own test holes beside Bre-X's." "That March" is March 1997, following line 2's "By nineteen ninety-seven". It is anchored to the story, not to the viewing date. If the line has to stand alone, use "In March nineteen ninety-seven, …" (+2 words).

### 5. "Nine days later, the chief geologist, Michael de Guzman, fell from a helicopter over the jungle."

- **March 19, 1997: confirmed.** Sources: Maclean's, May 19, 1997; AP, May 6, 1997 ("plunged to his death from a helicopter March 19"); the Reuters chronology; CBC on the FIA report ("died … March 19th after falling from a helicopter over the Indonesian jungle").
  - The CSM of May 7, 1997 says "March 18". That is the North American calendar date; the fall happened on the morning of March 19 in Borneo. Use March 19.
- **"Nine days later": needs correction.** It only works from March 10, which is unsupported. The real order is: Freeport drills (March) → de Guzman dies (Mar 19) → Freeport announces (Mar 26, seven days later).
- **Corrected line:** "On March nineteenth, chief geologist Michael de Guzman fell from a helicopter. A week later, the partner reported insignificant amounts of gold." "Over the jungle" was cut for length because line 2 already says jungle. Put it back (+3 words) if line 8 loses "To be fair,".

### 6. "On May 4th, independent testers confirmed it. The core samples had been salted, with gold dust bought from local panners."

- **May 4, 1997: confirmed.** Freeport's 8-K Ex. 99.1 (May 5, 1997): Freeport "received the Strathcona interim report late on the afternoon of May 4, 1997." AP, May 6, 1997: "reported Sunday" (May 4).
- **What the report found: confirmed.**
  - Strathcona's cover letter, as quoted by the CSM on May 7, 1997: "The magnitude of the tampering with core samples we believe has occurred … is of a scale and over a period of time and with a precision that, to our knowledge, is without precedent in the history of mining anywhere in the world."
  - Strathcona as quoted in Freeport's 8-K: "falsification and misrepresentation of many thousands of samples."
  - Strathcona as quoted by Maclean's: "the foreign gold that we and others before us have identified in the Bre-X samples."
  - AP: "doctored with gold from elsewhere."
- **"Bought from local panners": not what the May 4 report said.** Strathcona said the gold was *foreign*, probably added at Samarinda. The local-panner origin comes from later sources:
  - Porter GeoConsultancy tour notes, Oct–Nov 1998: "salted with alluvial gold acquired from artisanal miners."
  - Reuters, July 31, 2007: "salted with river gold … as well as with bits of shaved off jewelry."
  - The FIA report of 1997–98, as summarised in a Northern Miner commentary: alluvial gold "purchased from a local Dayak."

  Some geologists later disputed the alluvial reading (Northern Miner commentary). So don't pin the panners on the May 4 report.
- **"Testers" → "auditors":** Strathcona ran a "technical audit" (Freeport 8-K).
- **Corrected line:** "On May fourth, independent auditors confirmed it: the core samples had been salted with outside gold."

### 7. "Three days later, the shares were worth six cents. About forty thousand investors lost everything. Ontario's teachers alone lost a hundred million dollars."

- **Timing: needs correction.** Trading was halted Monday, May 5, and resumed **Tuesday, May 6, 1997**, two days after the May 4 report (AP, May 6, 1997).
- **Six cents: confirmed (C$, TSE).**
  - Business Standard, May 8, 1997 (FT copy): "Bre-X shares plunged to 6 Canadian cents when trading re-opened on Tuesday on the Toronto Stock Exchange. Although they recovered to 8 cents…"
  - AP, May 9, 1997: "closed at about 6 cents," a market value of "about $14 million."
  - The Militant, June 23, 1997, gives C$0.085.

  The closing price varies by source (6 to 8.5 cents); the plunge to 6 cents is agreed. Say "**sank to** six cents."
- **About 40,000: confirmed as an estimate.** Globe and Mail (Paul Waldie), Mar 21, 2013: "left roughly 40,000 Bre-X shareholders wondering what happened." CBC and Global News (2013) say only "tens of thousands."
  - **"Lost everything" goes too far.** They lost nearly all of their *Bre-X* holding (down 99.8% from the ~C$28 peak; down 99.6% from C$15.50), not everything they owned.
- **Ontario teachers, C$100M: confirmed.** Maclean's, May 19, 1997, and The Militant, June 23, 1997: "The Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan suffered a loss of $100 million." The loss was the *pension plan's*, not the teachers' own.
- **Bankruptcy (context):** Bre-X filed for court protection in Alberta on Thursday, **May 8, 1997** (AP, May 9, 1997). Wikipedia says May 9, which is the publication date. The TSE delisted the stock on May 7.
- **Corrected line:** "Two days later, the shares sank to six cents. About forty thousand shareholders were nearly wiped out. Ontario's teachers' pension alone lost a hundred million dollars."
- **On-screen:** `C$0.06 · TSE · May 6, 1997` · `≈40,000 shareholders (Globe and Mail estimate)` · `C$100M · Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan (Maclean's, May 19, 1997)`.

### 8. "To be fair, no one was ever convicted. The exploration chief, John Felderhof, sold eighty-four million dollars of stock in 1996, and was found not guilty of insider trading."

- **No one convicted: confirmed.**
  - The RCMP dropped its criminal probe in May 1999. The Northern Miner (May 1999) said police "have ceased active investigation." Reuters, July 31, 2007: "RCMP … said in 1999 it would not pursue charges … citing insufficient evidence."
  - Globe and Mail, Mar 21, 2013: "no one was ever charged with fraud." Felderhof was the only person prosecuted, and he was acquitted.
- **C$84M in 1996: confirmed (C$83.9M).** OSC, May 11, 1999: "in the period April 24, 1996 to September 10, 1996 Felderhof sold approximately 2,720,500 shares of Bre-X for approximately $83.9 million." The four lots add up to C$83,955,669. Reuters, July 31, 2007: "C$84 million … of Bre-X stock in 1996." These are TSE sales in C$.
  - Note: the Globe (2013) gives "$75-million" for the Felderhofs' share sales "between 1994 and 1996" (court filings). That is a different window and a different measure. Use the OSC figure.
- **Not guilty: confirmed, on JULY 31, 2007 (not Aug 9).** OSC release, July 31, 2007: "Judge Peter Hryn of the Ontario Court of Justice today found John Bernard Felderhof not guilty of four counts of insider trading and not guilty of four counts that he authorized, permitted or acquiesced in Bre-X issuing press releases in violation of Ontario securities law."
  - Wikipedia's "August 9, 2007" is wrong; the Reuters story it cites ran July 31.
  - The charges were under the Ontario Securities Act: insider trading (ss. 76(1) and 122(1)(c)) and misleading releases (s. 122(3)) (OSC, May 11, 1999).
  - Investment Executive's "Superior Court" is wrong; it was the Ontario Court of Justice.
- **"Exploration chief":** Felderhof was senior vice-president of exploration (AP, May 9, 1997) and later vice-chairman (OSC). OK.
- **Keep, with one optional trim:** "To be fair, no one was ever convicted. Exploration chief John Felderhof sold eighty-four million dollars of stock in nineteen ninety-six, and was found not guilty of insider trading."
- **On-screen:** `C$83.9M · Apr 24–Sep 10, 1996 (OSC)` · `NOT GUILTY · Ontario Court of Justice · July 31, 2007`.

### 9. "The lesson: a company's own results are not proof. Since 2001, Canadian miners need an independent expert to sign off on what they find."

- **In force Feb 1, 2001: confirmed.** OSC Bulletin notice, Jan 12, 2001: NI 43-101 "will come into effect on February 1, 2001"; s. 10.1 "shall come into force on February 1, 2001." The best-practice guidelines it relies on came from the TSE-OSC Mining Standards Task Force, set up after Bre-X (Companion Policy 43-101CP, s. 4.1).
- **"Independent expert": needs correction, because it overstates the rule.**
  - s. 2.1 (2001): all scientific or technical disclosure about a material property must be "based upon a technical report or other information prepared by or under the supervision of a **qualified person**."
  - s. 5.2: the qualified person signs the technical report.
  - s. 5.3: an **independent** qualified person is required only in certain cases: first-time reporting issuers, long-form prospectuses, first-time disclosure of resources or reserves, or a change of 100% or more. Producing issuers are exempt.
  - The 2011 revision keeps the same split: s. 2.1 says based on or approved by a qualified person; s. 5.3 sets out when an independent one is needed (OSC Bulletin, June 24, 2011).
  - A qualified person is an engineer or geoscientist with at least five years' experience who belongs to a professional association (s. 1.2).
- **Corrected line:** "The lesson: a company's own results aren't proof. Since two thousand one, Canadian miners need a qualified expert's sign-off, and often an independent one."
- **On-screen:** `NI 43-101 · in force Feb 1, 2001`.
