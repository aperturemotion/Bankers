A retired janitor in Vermont died in 2014. His will revealed he was worth almost $8 million.

Ronald Read grew up poor on a farm, served in World War II, pumped gas for about 25 years and then worked part-time as a janitor at JCPenney for 17 more. He drove a second-hand Toyota Yaris, held his coat together with a safety pin and chopped his own firewood into his nineties. For decades he quietly bought shares in companies he understood and held on to them. When he died in June 2014, aged 92, he owned at least 95 stocks, with the certificates stacked about five inches high in his safe deposit box.

He left $4.8 million to Brattleboro Memorial Hospital and $1.2 million to Brooks Memorial Library, about twice the library's yearly budget. In 2023 the hospital opened a new wing, the Ronald Read Pavilion, partly paid for by his gift.

To be fair, not every pick worked: he held Lehman Brothers when it collapsed in 2008, and his story is one man's result over seven decades, not a promise for anyone else.
